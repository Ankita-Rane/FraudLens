POLICY SOURCE use_for / not_for CROSS-CHECK FOR THESIS CITATION
================================================================

Cross-check date: 2026-09-02
Purpose: distinguish source-supported subject scope from researcher-imposed study
boundaries. Registry version 1.2 and the replacement canonical diagnostic implement
the corrections; version 1.1 and older diagnostics are preserved under old/.

IMPORTANT INTERPRETATION RULE
-----------------------------
The registry's use_for and not_for fields are metadata written by the researcher.
They are not quotations from CFPB, the Federal Reserve, NIST, or BIS. In the thesis,
cite the official source for what it covers. Describe not_for as a boundary imposed by
this study unless the source itself expressly states the limitation.

Do not write: "The source says it is not for fraud classification labels."
Safe wording: "The study uses the source only for [supported subject] and does not
treat it as authority for transaction-level classification or internal bank policy."


1. CFPB — 12 CFR 1026.12, Special credit card provisions
---------------------------------------------------------
Registry file: regulation_z_1026_12.html
Official URL: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/12/
Official page location: § 1026.12(b), "Liability of cardholder for unauthorized use";
official interpretations of § 1026.12(b).
Short source evidence: "Liability of cardholder for unauthorized use."

Registry use_for cross-check:
- "unauthorized credit-card use" — DIRECTLY SUPPORTED by § 1026.12(b)(1), which
  defines unauthorized use.
- "consumer liability context" — DIRECTLY SUPPORTED by § 1026.12(b), including the
  conditions and limits of cardholder liability.

Registry not_for cross-check:
- "institution-specific alert thresholds" — VALID STUDY BOUNDARY, but not an express
  statement by CFPB. The regulation addresses credit-card provisions and liability;
  it does not define this study's detector threshold.
- "fraud classification labels" — VALID STUDY BOUNDARY, but not an express CFPB
  statement. Do not cite § 1026.12 as a source of ULB/Sparkov labels or as proof that a
  transaction is fraudulent.

Thesis-safe description:
"Section 1026.12 was used as official regulatory context for unauthorized credit-card
use and cardholder-liability provisions. It was not used to determine detector
thresholds or transaction labels."

Suggested reference:
Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. (current version). 12 C.F.R. § 1026.12,
Special credit card provisions. Accessed 2 September 2026 from the URL above.


2. CFPB — 12 CFR 1026.13, Billing error resolution
---------------------------------------------------
Registry file: regulation_z_1026_13.html
Official URL: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/13/
Official page locations: § 1026.13(b), billing-error notice; § 1026.13(c), time for
resolution and general procedures; § 1026.13(e)-(f), resolution and investigation;
§ 1026.13(f)(2), documentary evidence on request.
Short source evidence: "Time for resolution; general procedures."

Registry use_for cross-check:
- "billing-error investigation procedure" — DIRECTLY SUPPORTED by § 1026.13(c),
  (e), and (f), including reasonable-investigation requirements.
- "timing and evidence context" — DIRECTLY SUPPORTED. Section 1026.13(b)(1) gives the
  notice period; § 1026.13(c) gives acknowledgement/resolution periods; § 1026.13(f)
  addresses explanations and documentary evidence.

Registry not_for cross-check:
- "institution-specific alert thresholds" — VALID STUDY BOUNDARY, not an express
  CFPB limitation.
- "fraud classification labels" — VALID STUDY BOUNDARY, not an express CFPB
  limitation. Billing-error procedures must not be presented as dataset ground truth.

Thesis-safe description:
"Section 1026.13 was used for official billing-error notice, investigation, timing and
documentary-evidence context. It was not used to set model thresholds or assign fraud
labels."

Suggested reference:
Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. (current version). 12 C.F.R. § 1026.13,
Billing error resolution. Accessed 2 September 2026 from the URL above.


3. Federal Reserve — SR 26-2, Revised Guidance on Model Risk Management
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Registry file: federal_reserve_sr_26_2.html
Official URL: https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602.htm
Official page locations: applicability paragraph; announcement paragraphs; attached
"Revised Guidance on Model Risk Management."
Short source evidence: "emphasize a risk-based approach to model risk management."

Registry use_for cross-check:
- "model governance" — SUPPORTED IN SUBSTANCE by the official letter's model-risk-
  management purpose and sound-principles language. "Model risk management" is more
  exact than "model governance" when citing the landing page.
- "validation" — NOT ESTABLISHED BY THE LOCALLY STORED LANDING-PAGE TEXT ALONE. It
  should be cited to the attached revised guidance, with a precise section/page, if it
  is used as a substantive claim.
- "controls and documentation" — TOO BROAD FOR THE LOCALLY STORED LANDING PAGE ALONE.
  Cite exact sections/pages of the attached guidance or narrow the registry wording.

Registry not_for cross-check:
- "transaction-level fraud decisions" — VALID STUDY BOUNDARY. The letter concerns
  model-risk-management principles and does not adjudicate individual transactions.
- "consumer legal advice" — VALID STUDY BOUNDARY, not an express sentence in the
  letter.

Applicability qualification that must appear when relevant:
The page says the letter is expected to be most relevant to Federal Reserve-regulated
banking organizations with more than $30 billion in total assets. Do not describe it
as universally applicable bank policy.

Registry correction implemented in version 1.2:
Replaced ["model governance", "validation", "controls and documentation"] with
["model risk management principles", "risk-based model risk management"] unless the
attached guidance is separately frozen and exact validation/control/documentation
sections are registered.

Thesis-safe description using the current local source:
"SR 26-2 was used as supervisory context for sound, risk-based model-risk-management
principles. It was not used to decide whether an individual transaction was fraud or
to provide consumer legal advice."

Suggested reference:
Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System. (2026, April 17). SR 26-2: Revised
Guidance on Model Risk Management. URL above. Cite the attached guidance separately
for any validation, control, or documentation requirement.


4. NIST AI 600-1 — Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile
--------------------------------------------------------------
Registry file: nist_ai_600_1.html
Official URL: https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence
DOI: https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1
Official page locations: abstract; publication keywords; the linked NIST AI 600-1
report for detailed practices.
Short source evidence: "cross-sectoral profile of and companion resource for the AI Risk Management Framework."

Registry use_for cross-check:
- "generative-AI risk" — DIRECTLY SUPPORTED by the title, abstract, report identity,
  and GAI-risk keywords.
- "measurement" — SUPPORTED IN SUBSTANCE by the page's AI measurement and evaluation
  classification and the report's relationship to the AI RMF.
- "governance controls" — BROAD PARAPHRASE. Prefer "GAI risk management and
  trustworthiness considerations" unless exact GOVERN function actions in the report
  are cited by action identifier/page.

Registry not_for cross-check:
- "transaction-level fraud decisions" — VALID STUDY BOUNDARY; NIST AI 600-1 is a
  cross-sectoral GAI risk-management profile, not a transaction classifier.
- "bank operating policy" — VALID STUDY BOUNDARY; the source is cross-sectoral and
  voluntary rather than an institution-specific operating policy.

Thesis-safe description:
"NIST AI 600-1 was used as a voluntary, cross-sectoral source for generative-AI risk
management and trustworthiness considerations. It was not treated as transaction-level
fraud authority or as a bank's operating policy."

Suggested reference:
Autio, C., Schwartz, R., Dunietz, J., Jain, S., Stanley, M., Tabassi, E., Hall, P., &
Roberts, K. (2024). Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative
Artificial Intelligence Profile (NIST AI 600-1). National Institute of Standards and
Technology. https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1


5. BIS Financial Stability Institute — FSI Insights 63
-------------------------------------------------------
Registry file: bis_fsi_insights_63.html
Official URL: https://www.bis.org/fsi/publ/insights63.htm
Current official landing URL: https://www.bis.org/publications/fsi-insight-63-regulating-ai-financial-sector-recent-developments-and-main-challenges
Official page locations: publication description/abstract and author disclaimer.
Short source evidence: "gen AI, which may give rise to hallucination and anthropomorphism risks."

Registry use_for cross-check:
- "financial-sector AI governance" — DIRECTLY SUPPORTED. The publication examines AI
  risks and regulatory implications in banking/insurance and identifies governance
  and model-risk management as areas needing attention.
- "hallucination and model-risk context" — DIRECTLY SUPPORTED by the official
  publication description.

Registry not_for cross-check:
- "transaction-level fraud decisions" — VALID STUDY BOUNDARY. The publication is
  sector-level research and supervisory analysis, not a transaction decision rule.
- "binding institution policy" — STRONGLY SUPPORTED AS A BOUNDARY. The page labels it
  an FSI publication and states that the authors' views do not necessarily represent
  the BIS, member central banks, or Basel-based standard setters.

Thesis-safe description:
"FSI Insights 63 was used for financial-sector AI-governance, model-risk and
generative-AI hallucination context. It was treated as research and supervisory
analysis, not as binding institution policy or transaction-level decision authority."

Suggested reference:
Crisanto, J. C., Leuterio, C. B., Prenio, J., & Yong, J. (2024). Regulating AI in the
financial sector: recent developments and main challenges (FSI Insights No. 63).
Financial Stability Institute, Bank for International Settlements. URL above.


SUMMARY OF REQUIRED THESIS WORDING CHANGES
------------------------------------------
1. Identify every not_for item as a study-imposed use boundary unless the source
   expressly supplies that limitation.
2. For SR 26-2, either freeze and cite the attached guidance by section/page or narrow
   use_for to model-risk-management principles and a risk-based approach.
3. For NIST, prefer "GAI risk management and trustworthiness considerations" over the
   unspecific phrase "governance controls" unless exact GOVERN actions are cited.
4. Do not imply that any of the five sources determines ULB/Sparkov labels, detector
   thresholds, legal outcomes, or transaction-level fraud.
5. Preserve access dates and snapshot hashes separately from bibliographic citations;
   a hash establishes snapshot integrity, not continuing legal applicability.
